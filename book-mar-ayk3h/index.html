This week, there was some pumpkin in the fridge, so I tried making a sandwich with it, and this pumpkin sandwich was more delicious than I imagined, which surprised me.

To bring out the pumpkin's sweetness, I added a little salt and pepper, mixed in mayonnaise, and put it between slices of bread.

It was my first time making a pumpkin sandwich, but it was so delicious that it looks like it will become one of my regulars from now on.

Just thinking about it like this now brings that delicious taste back to me.

DAY 28 · MARCH 28
Mexican Food, Japanese Hospitality目の前でアボカドを切ってくれるメキシコ料理店

Yesterday, I went out for lunch with a friend.

We both have birthdays in February, so this is a friend I celebrate with every February, but this year our schedules just wouldn't line up, and it ended up a little late.

This time, we went to a Mexican restaurant my friend recommended.

The restaurant was really popular, and right after we took our seats, it filled up in no time.

Recently, more and more restaurants use robots to bring out the food, but this one was different: they prepared the avocado right in front of us, the staff explained the dishes, and there was a lot of communication.

It's a Mexican restaurant, but I thought this kind of attentive service is something unique to Japan.

I'd like to go again.

DAY 29 · MARCH 29
Did You Know Eggs Have a Season?卵にも旬と上手な割り方がある

Did you know that eggs have a season?

According to the radio I was listening to this morning, eggs also have a season, and apparently this mild spring season is the season for delicious eggs.

After that, the radio program explained how to crack eggs well.

Apparently, when you crack an egg, instead of hitting it against an edge like the corner of a table, it's better to tap it lightly, tap-tap, on a flat surface like a cutting board.

Apparently, doing this keeps pieces of the shell from getting inside.

They also said that when you drop the egg out, you can do it neatly if you open it from the crack with the crack facing up, not down.

I thought, "I see," and planned to try it right away when making fried eggs for breakfast today, but I completely forgot, and by the time I noticed, the eggs were already sizzling in the frying pan.

I always crack eggs without thinking, out of habit, so next time I'll try to remember and give it a try.

DAY 30 · MARCH 30
I Sowed My Balcony Garden Againベランダのプランターに6種類の種をまく

The amount of pollen has been gradually decreasing, so this weekend I sowed vegetable seeds in the planters on my balcony.

Ideally, it's best to sow the seeds from around mid-February, but at that time it's still quite cold, and on top of that the pollen is flying, so I just couldn't bring myself to work on the balcony, and it ended up being this late.

The seeds I sowed in the planters this time are mini daikon radish, turnip, shungiku (garland chrysanthemum), mizuna, cilantro, and basil.

I also took the garlic chives, which keep growing healthily all year round, out of the planter once, loosened their roots, softened the soil, gave them nutrients, and took good care of them.

I had long been renting a shared farm plot to grow vegetables, but after I got shingles last year, taking care of the plot became hard, and I ended up giving up growing vegetables.

But as it gets warmer, I really do find myself wanting to touch the soil and enjoy watching vegetables grow from seeds.

DAY 31 · MARCH 31
A Night Walk Past Late Cherry Blossoms増上寺と東京タワーの夜桜散歩

Last night, on my way home from work, I went on a nighttime cherry blossom walk with a friend.

This year, even though it's already been about ten days since the official announcement that the cherry blossoms had started blooming, many places are still only 70 or 80 percent in bloom, so it feels a little anticlimactic.

The place we always go for our cherry blossom walks is Shiba Park, near Zojoji Temple and Tokyo Tower in Tokyo.

Zojoji has been under renovation since last year, so many areas are off-limits, and this year there were fewer lit-up cherry blossom spots.

But Somei Yoshino cherry blossoms are moody, so depending on the weather, you never know when they'll fall.

So as not to miss the Somei Yoshino's brief appearance that comes only once a year, we walked along the night streets looking up at the cherry blossoms.

The weather forecast says it will be windy today with rain as well, so this year's Somei Yoshino might end just one step before full bloom.

I want to enjoy these cherry blossoms so that I have no regrets.
